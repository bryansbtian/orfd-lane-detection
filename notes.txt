Lateral Position Estimation Under Partial Road Visibility
==========================================================

PROBLEM: When a forward-facing camera can't see both road edges (wide road,
tight curve, or narrow FOV), the computed "center" of visible pixels is
biased. The car thinks it's centered when it's actually off to one side.

RELEVANT PAPERS & APPROACHES
-----------------------------

1. Vanishing Point + Road Model Methods
   - Kong, Audibert, Ponce (2009) - "Vanishing Point Detection for Road Detection" - CVPR 2009
     Uses vanishing point as anchor to infer road geometry even when edges are clipped.
     VP is invariant to lateral position.

   - Lee et al. (2017) - "VPGNet: Vanishing Point Guided Network for Lane and Road Marking Detection" - ICCV 2017
     End-to-end network that jointly estimates vanishing point and lane markings.
     Provides geometric constraints when markings are partially visible.

2. Polynomial / Parametric Lane Models
   - Aly (2008) - "Real time Detection of Lane Markers in Urban Streets" - IEEE IV 2008
     Fits parametric curves (B-splines) to detected lane features; the parametric
     model extrapolates beyond visible edges.

   - Bar Hillel, Lerner, Levi, Raz (2014) - "Recent progress in road and lane detection: a survey"
     Machine Vision and Applications. Comprehensive survey covering model-based
     approaches that handle partial visibility through geometric priors.

3. Bird's Eye View (BEV) / Inverse Perspective Mapping (IPM)
   - In BEV, parallel lane boundaries can be extrapolated even when one edge
     leaves the frame. RANSAC polyfit is a step in this direction.

4. Deep Learning with Structural Priors
   - Neven et al. (2018) - "Towards End-to-End Lane Detection: Instance Segmentation Approach" - IEEE IV 2018
     LaneNet with learned embeddings; handles partial lanes.

   - Tabelini et al. (2021) - "Keep your Eyes on the Lane: Real-time Attention-guided Lane Detection" - CVPR 2021
     LaneATT - attention-based detection robust to partially visible lanes.

   - Liu et al. (2021) - "CondLaneNet: Top-to-Down Lane Detection Based on Conditional Convolution" - ICCV 2021
     Row-wise detection that naturally handles lanes entering/exiting the frame.

5. Ego-Lane Estimation
   - Borkar, Hayes, Smith (2012) - "Polar Randomized Hough Transform for Lane Detection
     Using Loose Constraints of Parallel Lines" - ICIP 2012
     Uses parallel line constraints to infer the missing edge.

   - Mammeri, Boukerche, Tang (2016) - "A model-based approach for lane change and merge prediction"
     Uses Kalman filtering of lateral offset.

PRACTICAL SOLUTIONS (ranked by feasibility for our setup)
----------------------------------------------------------

1. ASSUME KNOWN ROAD WIDTH
   - Track road width from frames where both edges are fully visible.
   - When one edge is clipped (left==0 or right==W-1), use:
       true_center = visible_edge +/- (known_width / 2)
   - Simplest, most effective for our case.

2. VANISHING POINT ANCHORING
   - VP gives road heading; combined with one visible edge, center can be
     triangulated. More complex but doesn't need width assumption.

3. TEMPORAL KALMAN FILTERING
   - Already implemented. Smooth lateral offset across frames where visibility
     alternates between full and partial.

4. SYMMETRY-BASED CORRECTION
   - Measure how much of each edge is visible (is left at 0? is right at W-1?)
   - Apply bias correction proportional to the asymmetry.

5. HOMOGRAPHY + PARALLEL LINE PRIOR (BEV)
   - Transform to bird's eye view where road edges are parallel.
   - Fit one edge, offset by estimated width to get the other.
   - Most robust but requires camera calibration / homography matrix.

OUR IMPLEMENTATION: Known-width correction (solution #1)
---------------------------------------------------------
Based on the lane width prior approach used across multiple papers above.
When both edges visible: track running average of road width.
When one edge clipped: estimate true center = visible_edge +/- (avg_width / 2).
This is the same geometric prior used in classical ADAS lane estimators.
